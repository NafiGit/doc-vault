Starts organised, stays yours

Doc Vault opens with School and University folders already laid out, with placeholders naming exactly which document belongs where — tap one and import the real file. After that it's your system: rename folders, drag them into your order, move files between them, add as many as you need.

Files stay files

PDFs open in your usual viewer, images in your gallery app. Sharing uses Android's standard share sheet and secure file links, so WhatsApp, Gmail and Drive all receive the real document — not a screenshot of it.

Light enough to forget

The whole app is about 16 MB, runs on any Android 8+ phone, and the bubble only appears when you switch it on — hide it any time from its notification.

Your certificates never leave your phone.

These are the most sensitive documents you own. Doc Vault treats them that way — and it's open source, so you don't have to take anyone's word for it.

Private by design

  • Local only. Documents live in the app's private storage on your device. There is no server.
  • No account, no analytics. Nothing is uploaded, tracked or phoned home — ever.
  • Files move only when you share one — into the exact chat or app you picked.
  • Fully open source. Every line is on GitHub — audit it, or build it yourself.

Straight talk — v0.1 limits

  • No PIN or fingerprint lock yet. It's on the roadmap — for now, rely on your phone's lock if you share your device.
  • No backup or sync. Uninstalling deletes the vault, so keep your originals backed up elsewhere.
  • One permission to grant: Android asks you to allow “Display over other apps” — that's the bubble, and only you can switch it on.

Fair questions.

How is this different from DigiLocker or Google Drive?

Those are places you go to. Doc Vault comes to you: the bubble floats over the app you're already in, so sharing a document is a two-tap detour instead of leaving the chat, opening another app, searching, downloading and coming back. It also works fully offline and needs no account.

Is it really free? What's the catch?

It's free and open source, built as a personal tool and shared as-is. There's no server to pay for — everything happens on your phone — so there's nothing to charge for and nothing to sell. The code is public on GitHub; contributions are welcome.

Why does it need “Display over other apps”?

That's the Android permission that lets the bubble float above WhatsApp and other apps — it's the whole point of the app. Android only lets you grant it yourself, in Settings, and you can revoke it any time. Doc Vault asks for no other sensitive permissions.

Which phones does it run on?

Any Android phone running Android 8.0 (Oreo) or newer. On some brands — Xiaomi, Oppo, Vivo, Samsung — there's an extra “floating window” toggle in the manufacturer's settings and a battery setting to set to unrestricted; the README on GitHub walks through both.

Can I organise it my way?

Yes. Beyond the School and University folders it starts with, you can add, rename and delete folders, drag them into your preferred order, and move files between folders. Most people end up with a Placements, Identity and Certificates setup within a week.

Is drag-and-drop into WhatsApp reliable?

Long-press and drag-out works on most recent WhatsApp versions, but it depends on WhatsApp accepting the drop. The share button is the guaranteed route — it uses Android's standard share sheet, and WhatsApp always appears there.
